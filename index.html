02 · ABSTRACT

Abstract

In simultaneous localization and mapping problem beyond pose estimation and geometric reconstruction, a good semantic understanding of the scene is also crucial part to support navigation, interaction, and decision-making. SLAM provides a key foundation for estimating of the system’s motion, position, and together with a representation of vehicle’s surroundings. In particular, self-driving cars or robots need a fine-grained understanding of the surfaces and objects in their vicinity. Geometric maps describe the spatial structure of the environment, while semantic information identifies the categories of its objects and regions. This additional context can support navigation, interaction, and decision-making by helping autonomous systems interpret their surroundings.

This thesis addresses the integration of semantic scene understanding into an efficient implicit SLAM pipeline. The objective is to exploit vision foundation model features during training while enabling semantic segmentation from LiDAR scans alone at inference time, and to incorporate the resulting predictions into an incrementally updated map. The proposed approach takes advantage of DINOv3 feature during training of a semantic segmentation model. Once trained, the model predicts semantic labels directly from LiDAR input, without requiring images for segmentation. The segmentation pipeline is integrated into PIN SLAM, where successive scans update the same map, allowing scene geometry and semantic categories to be queried and visualized together.

Our method on SemanticKITTI achieves a mean intersection over union (mIoU) of 64.13% on the validation set and 63.5% on the test set, with semantic segmentation inference running at 16.5 frames per second. On the IPB dataset validation split, the model trained on manually annotated local data achieves 56.87% mIoU. Compared with geometry-only mapping, the semantic configuration achieves lower aligned absolute pose error. The integrated system demonstrates live semantic map updates in PIN-SLAM using LiDAR input alone.
